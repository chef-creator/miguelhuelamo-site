01 /

Walking Around

The knife shops in Kappabashi are a pilgrimage. I went for one thing and left with three, wrapped like relics. A blade there is not a tool you buy, it is a relationship you begin.

02 /

Eating & Drinking

I landed with a list and abandoned it by the second morning. Tokyo does not reward the itinerary; it rewards the detour. The best meal of the trip was a counter I found by following a queue of construction workers at seven in the morning, a bowl of rice, grilled fish, miso and pickles that cost less than a coffee and taught me more than a tasting menu.

What strikes a chef here is the reverence for a single thing done completely. One man, one product, one lifetime. It is the opposite of the multi-outlet operation I run, and it recalibrates you.

The city taught me that mastery is mostly restraint. Do less, but refuse to do it badly.

03 /

Outside Tokyo

Hakone

From Kamakura to Hakone, train then bus, just the three of us. Mount Fuji came and went through the clouds, more suggestion than sight, which somehow made it better. We stayed a couple of days in an old ryokan, yukatas on, eating home-cooked meals we didn't have to think about. Time enough to visit a few temples, circle the lake, and let the trip slow down before Tokyo picked the pace back up.

I flew home heavier in the luggage and lighter in the head. A few knives, a lot of notes, and a reminder that the point of travelling for a cook is not to collect recipes. It is to be humbled by someone else's discipline and to carry a little of it back to your own pass.

Until the next coordinate lights up.
